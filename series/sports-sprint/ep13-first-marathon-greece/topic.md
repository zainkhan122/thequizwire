# Where was the FIRST marathon race run?

## Signal
suggest: YouTube autocomplete for "olympics first" → "first olympics athens 1896", "first olympics athens 1896 movie",
"first olympics in ancient greece" (2026-10-21, review/signals.md). Also wikipedia: "Marathon" sits in the long-tail of every
big-city marathon weekend (NYC marathon is 2026-11-01), when "why is a marathon 42.195 km" searches spike each year.

## Why this, why now
The NYC Marathon (1 Nov) puts "marathon" in feeds for a week; the fact that the first race was ~40 km and the odd 42.195 km
came from a British royal box in 1908 is a genuine "wait, what?". The route itself is the visual (Marathon → Athens), so the
Short can show the answer instead of saying it — a runner sticker rides the course as it draws.

## Viewer takeaway
"The marathon isn't 42 km because of Greece — it's because the 1908 London race had to finish in front of the royal box."

## Question shape
where-first (place question) — the answer is a *route on a map*, which a which-country shape would flatten. The three options are
all plausible birthplaces of the modern Olympics' most famous race.

## Options and why they fool
A Greece (answer) · B United Kingdom — the 42.195 km distance really was fixed in London 1908, so viewers who know half the story
pick this · C USA — Boston is the oldest annual marathon (1897), a genuine near-miss for anyone who knows running history.

## Search term
"why is a marathon 26.2 miles" (suggest: YouTube autocomplete for "why is a marathon", 2026-10-21 — the one phrasing runners actually type that carries no spoiler; "first olympics athens 1896" names the answer, so it stays out of the title)

## Rejected alternatives
- "Why is a marathon 42.195 km" as a direct question — stronger search phrase, but it has no map answer and no options; it is the explanation, not the quiz. Kept as the payoff.
- "Which country has won the most Olympic marathons" — Kenya/Ethiopia debate depends on men/women split; two-publisher rule gets messy.

## Copy
Title leads with the search term "why is a marathon 26.2 miles" (what runners type) and turns it toward the question, because the
distance mystery is what earns the click from runners, not the trivia; "40 km" stays out of all copy because it sits on a fact card. First line of the YouTube
description sells the twist without the answer (the 2 km "have nothing to do with where the race began"). Comment question is
specific: "which did you pick before the route drew itself" — the reveal device is the route. Tags are the subject's own
(#marathon, #olympichistory, #runningfacts) plus the format tag (#sportsquiz) and #shorts; Instagram goes to the running
community tags because that is who shares marathon content; Facebook asks "have you ever run one?" for stories. Left out:
#greece, #athens (spoilers), #fyp-style tags, and any "did you know" phrasing.
