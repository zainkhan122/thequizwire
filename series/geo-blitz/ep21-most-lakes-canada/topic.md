## Why this, why now
Everyone says Finland, "the land of a thousand lakes", or Russia because it is huge. Canada holds about 880,000 lakes above
0.1 sq km — roughly 62 % of all such lakes on Earth, more than every other country combined. The why is the ice age (the
Laurentide sheet scraped the Shield bare and meltwater filled the hollows) and the kicker is scale: Great Bear and Great Slave,
two of the ten greatest lakes on the planet, sit where almost nobody lives. A count question, not a record — different from
#17's "deepest" and #19/#20's "coldest".

## Viewer takeaway
"Canada has more lakes than the rest of the world combined — about 880,000 — because an ice sheet carved them."

## Rejected alternatives
- "Which country has the most islands" (approved row 1) — Statistics Sweden says 267,570 but Norway's mapping authority now counts
  320,249; the two publishers disagree on who is first, so it fails the two-publisher rule. Lakes has one dataset (HydroLAKES)
  every publisher cites.
- "Which country has the most volcanoes" — depends on active vs total (Indonesia vs USA); same problem.
- "Deepest lake" — already #17 (Baikal).
