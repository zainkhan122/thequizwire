## Why this, why now
The anecdote is the hook: in 1816 at the Necker Hospital in Paris, René Laennec, reluctant to press his ear to a young
woman's chest, rolled a sheet of paper into a tube and heard her heart more clearly than ever (Roguin 2006; Britannica). He
named it from the Greek for chest and look, published in 1819, and died of tuberculosis — the disease his tube diagnosed
best — at 45. Body & Brain #10 is American; this returns the series to Europe with a French answer and a new device set.
