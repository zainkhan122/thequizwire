# The land nobody wants (Bir Tawil)

## Signal
wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Tawil ("one of the only habitable places not claimed by any recognised
government" — a perennial top trivia search); suggest: "land that no country wants" (YouTube autocomplete under
"unclaimed land", checked 2026-11-18); news: the Hala'ib dispute resurfaces every time Egypt–Sudan relations hit the
headlines. Our catalogue has the Halaib plate in ep10 (Nile basin) but no story about why the gap exists.

## Why this, why now
Everyone has heard "there is land nobody wants"; almost nobody has seen the two lines that cause it in one frame. The
1899 Condominium boundary (22°N) and the 1902 administrative boundary disagree; each state asserts the line that
hands it the 20,580 km² coastal triangle, which means neither can claim the 2,060 km² inland patch. It is a border
story that resolves visually, and it exercises the border-policy plates the stories format must get right from day one.

## Viewer takeaway
"Bir Tawil (about 2,060 km²) is claimed by neither Egypt nor Sudan because the 1899 line (22°N) and the 1902
administrative line disagree, and each country asserts the line that gives it the far larger Hala'ib Triangle on the
Red Sea, which Egypt administers and Sudan claims."

## Question shape
why-here-not-there — two lines, two outcomes; cold open "nobody claims it", wide-drift opener along the 22nd parallel,
both lines drawn as routes, the coastal triangle lit as the prize, the small patch as the void, to-scale bars for size.

## Twist
Refusing the land is rational: claiming Bir Tawil means accepting the line that loses the coast. The close returns to
the opening noun ("unclaimed on purpose").
