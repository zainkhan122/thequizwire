## Why this, why now
Before 16 October 1846 surgery meant being held down. That day, in the amphitheatre of Massachusetts General Hospital in
Boston, dentist William Morton put Gilbert Abbott to sleep with ether and surgeon John Warren cut a tumour from his neck
(MGH; Linda Hall Library). The twist: Crawford Long of Jefferson, Georgia had done it in 1842 and never published (The
Conversation; Britannica). The closer: within two months Robert Liston amputated a leg under ether in London (RCS; UCL).
Both rival firsts are American, so the dispute does not touch the answer.
