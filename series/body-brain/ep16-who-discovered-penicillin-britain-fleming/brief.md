# Brief — Body & Brain #16 · Which country DISCOVERED penicillin?

## Hook fact
Alexander Fleming, bacteriologist at St Mary's Hospital, London, came back from holiday on 3 September 1928 and noticed a
Penicillium mould had killed the staphylococci around it on a culture plate; he published in 1929 but could not purify the
substance (Nobel Prize Outreach; ACS Landmark; Britannica). Howard Florey, Ernst Chain and Norman Heatley at Oxford made it
a usable drug in 1940 (mice, Lancet August 1940); the first patient, policeman Albert Alexander, was treated on 12 February
1941, improved, and died when the supply ran out. US industry mass-produced it from 1941; public from March 1945. The 1945
Nobel Prize went to Fleming, Chain and Florey; Heatley got nothing (Science Museum Group; Wikipedia: History of penicillin).
