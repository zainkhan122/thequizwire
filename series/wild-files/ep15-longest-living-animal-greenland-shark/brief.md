# Brief — Wild Files #15 · Which of these animals lives the LONGEST?

## Hook fact
The Greenland shark is the longest-lived vertebrate known: the largest female in Nielsen et al. (Science, 2016) was dated at
392 ± 120 years by eye-lens radiocarbon; sexual maturity at least 156 ± 22 years; growth about 1 cm a year.

## Show it
Night-lights opener over the dark Atlantic at k 0.5 under the question; three emoji options (whale, tortoise, shark). Reveal =
flash-cut onto Greenland with a cold-blue lift, the tag "Greenland shark · ~400 yr" and the ~400 YEARS card. Ease back over
the North Atlantic for the age table: three columns — shark 392, bowhead 200, tortoise 190 — with a whale sticker on "beats" and an
eye sticker on "lens" (how the age was read). Push north-east into the sea between Greenland and Svalbard: a ruler sticker on
"grows", the "Arctic waters · near freezing" tag and a snowflake on "water", and a counter that creeps to 150 on "adult".
Drift back to Greenland's coast: shark sticker, then a SHARK / USA split — 1744 against 1776 — the highlight pulsing on "existed".

## Different from
#14 (peregrine) was a compare chart + a rise photo + a flash; #13 lions bars + pins; #12 polar bears a two-pole route with six
pins. This one has no pins and no photo: a time story told with a three-column compare, a creeping counter and a date split, over a
single polar camera journey — the first Wild Files episode whose split compares years, not animals.
