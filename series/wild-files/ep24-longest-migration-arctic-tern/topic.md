## Why this, why now
The reflex answers are the big travellers everyone has seen on television: the humpback whale and the monarch
butterfly. The record is a 100-gram seabird: tracked Arctic terns from Greenland and Iceland average about 70,900 km a
year on an S-shaped Atlantic route to the Antarctic ice and back (Egevang et al., PNAS 2010). It sees two summers a
year and in a 30-year life covers about 2.4 million km, three return trips to the Moon (Audubon; Britannica). A route
you can draw from the top of the map to the bottom, with a whale's route beside it for scale.
