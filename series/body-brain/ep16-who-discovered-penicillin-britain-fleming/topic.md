# Which country DISCOVERED penicillin?

## Signal
suggest: "who discovered penicillin" (YouTube autocomplete under "penicillin", checked 2026-11-20); wikipedia:
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_penicillin; news: antimicrobial-resistance week (18–24 November) puts the first
antibiotic back in every explainer. Our catalogue has the first X-ray (#14), the stethoscope (#11) and anaesthesia (#12) but
nothing on antibiotics; nothing in the last five episodes shares the "which country discovered" stem.

## Why this, why now
Everyone knows Fleming and the dish. Almost nobody knows the gap: the mould was spotted in London in 1928 and was useless
for thirteen years because nobody could make enough; the first patient improved and died when the supply ran out; an
Oxford team made it a drug in 1940, America built the factories, and the 1945 Nobel left out the man who ran the production.
One real wartime photograph rises out of Britain; a FOUND / MADE split carries the two dates.

## Viewer takeaway
"Penicillin was discovered in Britain in 1928 at St Mary's Hospital, London, but it took until 1940 at Oxford to become a
drug and until the war for America to mass-produce it; the first patient died when the supply ran out; Fleming, Florey
and Chain shared the 1945 Nobel Prize and Heatley was left out."
