# The navy with no sea (Bolivia)

## Why this, why now
A navy without a sea is a one-line hook that everybody doubts and a map answers in under a minute: the shore Bolivia
held (about 400 km around Antofagasta), the 1879 war that took it, the 1904 treaty, the court that said no in 2018,
and the lake at 3,800 m where the sailors actually train. It is the ideal first Geo Story: one subject country, one
neighbour, one route, one lake, no disputed borders to caption.

## Viewer takeaway
"Bolivia lost its Pacific coast to Chile in the War of the Pacific (1879–84, treaty 1904), still marks the Day of the
Sea, lost its 2018 World Court case, and keeps a navy of about 5,000 on Lake Titicaca and its rivers."

## Question shape
the-edge-case — a country that keeps an institution its geography no longer supports; cold-open "Bolivia has a navy"
on the map, plunge opener over the Altiplano, the lost shore drawn as a route, the lake as the twist.

## Twist
The navy is real and sizeable (about 5,000 personnel) but it sails on a mountain lake nearly four kilometres up, not on
any sea; the close returns to the opening noun ("a navy without a sea").
