# Which river crosses the Equator TWICE?

## Signal

Congo was the distractor nobody picked there — this time it is the answer.

## Why this, why now
Everyone reaches for the Amazon, the river of the Equator in the mind's eye. But the Amazon runs a few degrees south
of the line and never touches it; the Congo's great northern bend carries it over the Equator near Kisangani and back
across it near Mbandaka (Britannica; NASA Earth Observatory). It is also the deepest river measured, more than 220 m in
the lower gorge (USGS 2009), and ends past the closest pair of capitals in the world, Kinshasa and Brazzaville. A
"which river" stem with a line-of-latitude twist keeps the opening off the which-country run.

## Viewer takeaway
"The Congo is the only big river to cross the Equator twice — north then south — because of its giant bend; it is the
deepest river on Earth (over 220 m); Kinshasa and Brazzaville face each other across it, the closest capitals anywhere."
