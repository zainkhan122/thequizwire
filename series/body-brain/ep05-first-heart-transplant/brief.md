# Brief — Body & Brain #5 · The FIRST human heart transplant happened in which country?

## Hook fact
Not the United States: Christiaan Barnard did it at Groote Schuur Hospital, Cape Town, on 3 December 1967. The patient lived 18 days — and the heart he got beat with no nerves connecting it to his brain.

## Show it
An Atlantic-wide Mercator stage holding all three guesses (USA, South Africa, UK); the camera hops to each as it is named. Reveal = South Africa lifts in heart-red, "South Africa" tag, Cape Town · Groote Schuur pin, date card. Then a day counter climbs to 18 and stops on "pneumonia"; the camera pulls out, the United States lights up for Stanford's research, and Barnard's 1968 press-conference photograph (CC0, Nationaal Archief) rises out of South Africa on "first". Last beat: anatomical-heart and brain stickers, the NO NERVES card, and the heart throbs on "pacemaker".

## Beat order
1. s04 reveal — colour-lift South Africa, tag, Cape Town pin on "Cape", date card.
2. s05 counter — 0 → 18 "days he lived", frozen on "pneumonia".
3. s06 wide — USA second-highlight + "Stanford · research" tag on "Stanford"; Barnard photo rises on "first".
4. s07 body fact — 🫀 on "nerve", 🧠 on "brain", NO NERVES card on "own", heart throbs on "pacemaker".

## Different from
Body & Brain #4 (tallest men) used a compare chart + rank counter + night-lights opener. This one has a colour lift, a stopped counter, a second-highlight across an ocean, a rising photo and a heartbeat sticker — no chart, no compare, and it is the first medical-history map story in the series.

## Swipe test
- 1 s — satellite stage already moving under the question; the word FIRST in yellow.
- 6 s — the third camera hop lands on South Africa and the timer runs; most viewers have said "USA" out loud.
- 19 s — the United States suddenly lights up while the voice says "Americans had done the research" — the moment the wrong guess gets explained.
