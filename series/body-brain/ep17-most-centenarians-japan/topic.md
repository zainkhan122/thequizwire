# Where do the MOST people aged 100 or more live?

## Signal
trends: "centenarians" spikes every September around Japan's Respect for the Aged Day release (Google Trends, worldwide,
5 years, checked 2026-11-20); wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenarian; our stats (dispatch/stats.md): Body
& Brain "most per person" Shorts (#8 doctors, #13 coffee, #15 tea) hold well and #4 (tallest people) is the series'
best — population superlatives with a per-person twist are the proven engine.

## Why this, why now
The reflex answers are the biggest populations: China, or the United States. Japan has nearly 100,000 people aged 100
or more — 99,763 in September 2025, about 88% women, up from 153 when counting began in 1963 (Ministry of Health, Labour
and Welfare). Per person that is about 80 per 100,000 against roughly 30 in America and under 5 in China (US Census;
UN WPP). The silver-cup story (downgraded to silver-plated in 2016 because there were too many) is the human ending.

## Viewer takeaway
"Japan has the most centenarians — nearly 100,000, 9 in 10 women; 153 in 1963; about 80 per 100,000 people versus 30
in the US; the government used to send each one a silver cup."

## Question shape
which-country superlative with a per-person twist ("where do the most people aged 100 or more live?"); flag
options; reads verbatim as the hook; hook = misconception (the biggest population is not the answer); night-lights
opener over South-East Asia so Japan stays off-frame.

## Options and why they fool
A United States — the big rich country, and in raw numbers it is second · B China — ten times Japan's population,
the "most people" reflex · C Japan (answer) — the country everyone knows for long lives yet rarely picks over China.
